Anatomy is a surface. The landmarks that define a vertebra's coordinate frame — endplates, pedicles, processes — lie on its outer surface, yet the tools for labelling them work on volumes, slice by slice. Lithium labels anatomy as surface point clouds instead: 32,000 points per bone rather than hundreds of thousands of voxels. It is a single desktop application in which the catalog is the dataset, every stroke is persisted, Point Transformer models are trained in the loop, and the trained model runs back on the next bone. Interaction stays inside one frame at 1 M points (pick 27 ms, lasso 37 ms). On Gold247 — 247 hand-labelled VerSe vertebrae from 12 subjects — two models trained inside the app reach endplate precision of 0.941/0.920 on 38 held-out bones, and the vertebral frame derived from predicted labels agrees with the frame from hand labels to 0.39° (median). Pedicle precision (0.628) is the open gap. The annotation-timing study a tools paper owes is designed here and not yet run; its slot is left visibly empty. Lithium is open source (MIT).
