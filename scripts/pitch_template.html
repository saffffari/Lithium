Sheets · what it is

A workstation that labels anatomy on the surface and trains the model in the same window.

Lithium takes a CT-derived vertebra as a point cloud, lets you paint endplates, pedicles and processes directly on it, trains a Point Transformer on those labels, and runs the model back on the next bone. One app, one workstation, no cloud.

Train · what it produces

Two models trained inside the app, scored on subjects it never saw.

Yamato is Point Transformer V3 from scratch; Sonata is the same backbone fine-tuned from Meta's self-supervised weights. Both were trained from the Light Table's labels and scored on the {{N.val}} validation bones of two held-out subjects.

Why you · a surgeon's tool

Why a spine surgeon's name belongs on this.

Lithium is not an image-processing project that happens to touch the spine. Its ontology, its output and its open problems are all surgical. The name on it should be the person who decides what the anatomy means.

01 · ontology

It encodes your definition of a vertebra.

Endplates and pedicles are the SRS three-dimensional frame. Lithium teaches a model to find exactly those surfaces — not voxels, not a bounding box. A surgeon's authorship says the anatomy is right.

Stokes 1994 · SRS 3-D terminology
02 · throughput

Minutes per bone is what makes the dataset exist.

Labelled data is the bottleneck of every spine-AI paper. 247 bones are done; 15,000 are in flight. Residents can label in the Light Table; you sign off the conventions, not the strokes.

timing study designed, not yet run — the honest gap
03 · output

It ends at a measurement, not a segmentation.

From labels to a local frame is one function; from frames to disc angles, axial rotation and a 3D deformity classification is the pipeline already built around it.
